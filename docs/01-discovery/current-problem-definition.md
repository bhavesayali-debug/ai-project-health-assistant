# Current Problem Definition

## 1. Purpose

This document captures the current problem faced by Project Managers when assessing and communicating overall project health.

At this stage, the focus is on understanding the existing problem and current-state process before defining an AI or technology solution.

---

## 2. Project Manager Goal

A Project Manager needs to maintain an accurate and current understanding of overall project health.

This includes:

- Understanding whether the project is progressing as expected
- Identifying emerging risks, issues, blockers, and dependencies
- Understanding potential impact on project milestones
- Identifying areas requiring attention or escalation
- Communicating accurate status to stakeholders and leadership
- Supporting timely project decisions

The PM ultimately needs to answer:

> **Is the project healthy, what is changing, what needs attention, and is any intervention or escalation required?**

---

## 3. Current Sources of Project Information

Information required to assess project health may be distributed across multiple systems and communication channels, including:

- Jira
- Sprint updates
- Confluence
- SharePoint
- Defect tracking systems
- Risk registers
- Issue registers
- Project schedules
- Milestone plans
- Action-item trackers
- Dependency trackers
- Change requests
- Meeting notes
- Email
- Microsoft Teams or other collaboration platforms
- Stakeholder updates
- Team discussions

Each source may contain only part of the overall project picture.

---

## 4. Current-State Process

To determine project health, the Project Manager typically performs the following activities:

**Collect Project Information**

↓

**Review Updates**

↓

**Compare Information Across Sources**

↓

**Correlate Related Information**

↓

**Identify Risks, Issues, Blockers, and Dependencies**

↓

**Assess Impact on Milestones and Delivery**

↓

**Determine Overall Project Health**

↓

**Prepare Consolidated Status / Dashboard**

↓

**Communicate Status to Stakeholders**

↓

**Escalate Concerns When Required**

A significant part of this process depends on the PM manually connecting information generated by different teams, systems, and communication channels.

---

## 5. Example of the Problem

Project information may appear independently across different sources.

### Jira

> API integration task is delayed.

### Meeting Notes

> The vendor has still not provided the required API access.

### Project Schedule

> UAT is scheduled to begin next week.

Individually, these are separate updates.

The Project Manager must correlate them and determine that:

> **The vendor dependency is delaying API integration, which could place the upcoming UAT milestone at risk.**

This type of correlation is an important part of project-health monitoring.

---

## 6. Key Pain Points

### Fragmented Information

Project information is distributed across multiple tools, documents, meetings, and communication channels.

### Manual Consolidation

The PM spends time gathering, reviewing, and consolidating information before determining overall project status.

### Information Overload

Projects may generate large volumes of updates while only a small number require immediate PM attention.

### Conflicting Information

Different sources may provide inconsistent views of the same project activity.

### Stale Information

Formal trackers may not always contain the latest information discussed in meetings, email, or collaboration tools.

### Hidden Dependencies

A delay in one team or workstream may affect another milestone without the relationship being immediately obvious.

### Weak Signals

Emerging concerns may appear in meeting notes, emails, sprint discussions, or stakeholder comments before they are formally documented as risks or issues.

### Manual Correlation

Understanding overall project health depends heavily on the PM manually connecting information across different sources.

---

## 7. Potential Impact

If important project signals are overlooked or identified too late, the impact may include:

- Incorrect project-health reporting
- A project being reported as Green when underlying indicators suggest Amber or Red
- Delayed escalation
- Missed or delayed milestones
- Risks turning into active issues
- Unmanaged dependencies
- Stakeholder surprises
- Decisions based on incomplete or outdated information

---

## 8. Core Problem Statement

> **Project Managers need to continuously connect and interpret information from multiple project sources to understand the true health of a project and identify concerns early. The fragmented and continuously changing nature of project information makes this process time-consuming and increases the possibility that important risks, dependencies, inconsistencies, or emerging delivery concerns may be identified too late.**

---

## 9. Initial Project-Health Areas

For the first prototype, the project-health assessment will primarily focus on:

- Schedule and milestones
- Sprint progress
- Risks
- Issues
- Blockers
- Dependencies
- Defects
- Action items
- Scope/change signals
- Stakeholder concerns

Detailed budget and financial management will remain outside the initial prototype scope.
